Connection

/ws/arm

Connects to the FastAPI backend on the Pi. The page sends it the target with the speed and acceleration from Move; the backend solves it, moves the arm along a clear route and reads positions back. The backend also solves for the simulation, so the password is needed even without the arm.

    Playback

    simulation
    Pick a recording, a sequence or a motion below
    Rate1×

    Playback moves to the start pose at Max speed from Move, then follows the recording. With the timing kept, each joint gets the speed it needs (up to 150°/s); otherwise every joint uses Max speed and may lag. The whole path is collision-checked before it starts. When connected it runs on the arm itself, so it keeps going if you close this page. Stop or Esc ends it, and so does moving the target.

    Attachment

    Length
    Diameter

    The attachment is checked as a cylinder of its size along the flange axis: against the table (only its tip may come close), the base and shoulder, and the upper arm and forearm, so the wrist can't fold it back into the arm. Solving, the Move target and playback all use it, and so does the backend once connected. The target becomes the attachment's tip. Show its envelope from View in the 3D view.

    Work area

    Direction
    Width
    Max reach
    Base clearance

    The green slice on the floor. The tool tip (the target point: the flange centre, or the attachment's tip) must stay inside it; the rest of the arm may cross its edges. The tip also keeps clear of the base (up to 25 cm high): reaching in that close makes the arm fold its wrist back onto itself. Poses that take the tip outside are refused like a collision, on this page and by the backend. Directions are seen from above: 0° is the front, where the flange points at the zero pose (away from the Pi's ports); −90° is the arm's right.

    Safety

    The stall check stops the arm when a joint stays well short of its goal without moving, which usually means something is in the way.

    Calibration

    needs the arm

    It walks you through the steps below with the 3D model, and re-centres servos whose wrap point is in the way. Or do it by hand:

    1. Turn on Hand-guide (top bar) and pose the arm like the sim's zero pose.
    2. Set the zero.
    3. Bend each joint by hand: if the green pose turns the other way, reverse that joint.
